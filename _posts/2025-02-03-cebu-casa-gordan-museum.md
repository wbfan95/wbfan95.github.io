## App-guided 解说词

### Stop 1

Maayong Adlaw! Welcome to Casa Gorordo Museum! I am so glad that you dropped by for a visit. My name is Pepit and I am a tagbalay or resident of this house. I am excited to give you an insider’s tour of my household and introduce you to other family members who once lived here. As we go about my dwelling, I will tell you stories about the society and culture of Cebu I knew. I am very proud of my Cebuano roots! Many of the things you see, sense, and feel today in Cebuano Culture find their beginnings in the time Casa Gorordo was still young. I am grateful that Ramon Aboitiz Foundation Inc. had the vision to turn Casa Gorordo into a Museum A people like the Cebuanos, with people with so rich a culture needs to have a visible reminder of their story so that they can share it with pride to other people. You will see in the panels to your left a short history of Casa Gorordo This house was built when the Philippines was still a colony of Spain. My family moved here in 1863 The same year that Cebu's Port was reopened to world trade It is located in the old Parian district of Cebu. In our next stop, we will get to know more about the roles of Parian and its families in the historical transformation of Cebu. Casa Gorordo Museum Is classified as a National Historical Landmark for itst social and historical importance not only for Cebu but to the entire Philippines this is the first museum in Cebu to be opened for public enjoyment and education. I'll give you a few minutes to read through the panels. And then, let's move on to the 3D maps section. 

大家好！欢迎来到Casa Gorordo博物馆！非常高兴你们来参观。我叫Pepit，是这座房子的主人之一。今天，我将带领大家深入了解我们家的历史，并介绍那些曾在这里生活过的家族成员。在参观过程中，我会与大家分享我所知道的宿务的社会与文化故事。我为自己是宿务人感到自豪！你们今天在宿务文化中看到的许多风貌，都可以追溯到Casa Gorordo建成初期的时光。我十分感激拉蒙·阿博伊蒂兹基金会（Ramon Aboitiz Foundation Inc.）的远见，他们将这座房子改造为博物馆，成为宿务文化的传承者。宿务人拥有着如此深厚的文化底蕴，我们需要有一个象征性的存在，来铭记和传承我们的故事，让我们能自豪地与世界分享。左侧面板上有关于Casa Gorordo的简要历史。此房建于菲律宾还是西班牙殖民时期。我的家族于1863年迁入，这一年也是宿务港重新开放，迎来世界贸易的时刻。房子位于宿务的老Parian区。接下来，我们将一起了解Parian区及其家族在宿务历史变革中的重要角色。Casa Gorordo博物馆被列为国家历史地标，它不仅对宿务，对于整个菲律宾都具有重要的社会与历史意义。这是宿务的第一座开放的博物馆，供公众参观和学习。我会给大家一些时间阅读面板内容，之后我们将继续参观3D地图区。

### Stop 2

Did you know that Parian, the place where Casa Gorordo is located was a separate pueblo or town from, the city for three Centuries? let us travel back in time to as far as 400 years ago by looking at these 3D maps. They show how Parian and the City of Cebu evolved over centuries. Let's begin in the year 1614 when a parish was created for the immigrant Chinese Community who settled in what is now the Parian area. Back then, it was an estuary filled with mangroves, palms, and other plant and tree species that thrive in a marshy environment water from the hills, and mountains drained into this area and mingled with the salty sea. The rivers in this area were the entry points of merchants who traded with communities living along the coasts and inland. they brought in goods on small boats called sampans from their gigantic trading vessels. A marketplace grew on the banks of Parian River. The road that ran along the riverside market became known as the oldest street in the Philippines - Colon The word "Parian" is thought to mean "marketplace" and is believed to be derived from a native American Language, and introduced here by Spanish Settlers from Mexico. What an appropriate name for our old town for trading is second nature to us! 

你知道吗，Casa Gorordo所在的Parian（帕里安）曾是一个独立的城镇，与宿务市分开整整三百年。让我们通过这张3D地图，带着时光机回到400年前，看看Parian和宿务市是如何随着时间的流逝而发展的。我们从1614年开始，那一年为定居在如今Parian地区的华人移民设立了教区。当时，这片区域是一个河口，生长着红树林、棕榈树以及其他适应湿地环境的植物和树木。山上的水流汇入这里，与咸海水交织在一起。这里的河流是商人们进出贸易的通道，他们将货物从巨大的贸易船只通过小船（sampans）运送到岸边。在Parian河畔，逐渐形成了一个繁忙的市场。沿着这个市场的河岸道路，成为了菲律宾最古老的街道——科隆街（Colon）。而“Parian”这个名字，意指“市场”，据信源自一种美洲土著语言，由西班牙殖民者从墨西哥带入。这个名字真是为我们这座古老的商业重镇量身定制，因为经商已成为我们生活的自然一部分！

### Stop 3

Do you see that huge church? It was the pride of Parian it was built from contributions by families living here. When I was a little girl, I heard tales about how grand and beautiful it was from people who were able to set eyes on it before it was torm down. they say it was even bigger than the city's Cathedral! You may wonder why it was demolished it's a bit complicated to explain but suffice it to say, it was a result of a years long quarrel over the ownership of lands between church authorities and Parian, which did not end well for the families here despite their influence. It was decided by the colonial courts that the parish of San Juan Bautista of Parian, along with its church, be dissolved It’s a sad story but I guess nothing says permanent in this world even for such a well-made edifice as our Parian Church. Out of many beautiful houses and buildings that you see here from this era, only a very few remain… You can see in this map the other two houses in parian that are still standing the Jesuit house, and the house at the corner of our street owned by the Yap-Sandiego family Colon Street during this time was a busy area lined with shop houeses and other businesses. Only Photographs of that old Colon can be found.

你看见那座宏伟的教堂了吗？它曾是帕里安的骄傲，建造时得到了这里居民的捐赠。我小时候常听人们讲，许多在教堂拆除前曾亲眼见过它的人，都说它有多么壮丽美丽。有传闻说，它甚至比宿务市的大教堂还要宏伟！你也许会好奇，为什么它会被拆除？原因有些复杂，但简而言之，这是因为教会当局与帕里安之间的土地争议长达数年，最终尽管当地家庭有着很大的影响力，结果并不理想。殖民法院决定解散圣胡安·巴蒂斯塔教区和它的教堂。这是一个令人悲伤的故事，但或许世上没有什么是真正永恒的，就连像我们的帕里安教堂这样雄伟的建筑也未能幸免。在这个时代的众多美丽房屋和建筑中，至今能幸存下来的寥寥无几……在这张地图上，你可以看到仍然矗立在帕里安的另外两座房子——耶稣会修道院和位于我们街角、由 Yap-Sandiego 家族拥有的房子。那时的科隆街是一个热闹的商业街区，两旁商铺林立，生意兴隆。如今，那个时期的科隆街，只有老照片能为我们留下记忆。

### Stop 4

The third layer shows Parian and the City in the 1920's. What is important about this period? That's right! Cebu was already under Americal rule Our city had survived a difficult transformation marked by revolutionary wars calamities, epidemics, and starvation Cebuano society was forever changed by the coming of modern means of living trains and motorized transport sped up travel, electricity and waterworks gave us further convenience, and most of all government and education became a public business. Some people today look back at this period up to the 1930s with nostalgia and refer to it as "peacetime". My hometown, Parian, was by then, completely absorbed into the city. The center of commerce moved to Magallanes St., closer to the port area. All in all of these changes Parian families grew in stature and influence. They dominated politics not only in Cebu but in other parts of the Philippines as they expanded out of their traditional neighborhood. It seemed that whatever power they lost in that bitter dispute with colonial authorities in the previous century they took back and grew a hundredfold They got elected to positions in the national assembly and some were appointed to head important government offices. Later, I will introduce you to some well-known families who trace back their origins to this pueblo. 

第三层展示了1920年代的Parian（帕里安）和宿务市。这个时期有什么特别之处呢？没错，宿务当时已经处于美国统治下。我们的城市经历了艰难的转型，曾经遭遇革命战争、自然灾害、流行病和饥荒等多重考验。宿务社会因为现代化生活方式的到来，发生了深刻的变化。火车和汽车使得旅行更加便捷，电力和自来水的普及大大提高了我们的生活便利，而最重要的是，政府和教育逐渐成为公众事务。直到今天，许多人仍然怀念这一时期，甚至把它称为“和平时代”，直到1930年代。我故乡的Parian（帕里安），那时已完全融入宿务市的范围。商业中心迁移到更靠近港口的Magallanes街。尽管发生了这些变化，Parian的家族却日益崛起，他们的地位和影响力不断增强。他们主导着政治，不仅在宿务，在菲律宾其他地区也有着广泛的影响力，逐渐超越了他们传统的居住地。看起来，他们在上世纪与殖民政府的争斗中失去的权力，在这一时期不仅恢复了，还迅速扩展了百倍。他们当选为国会成员，还有一些被任命为政府重要职务的负责人。稍后，我会向大家介绍一些有着深厚历史渊源的知名家族，他们的根源可以追溯到这个古老的镇区。

### Stop 5

The final layer of this map shows what happened to our city during the Second World War. Over three years of Japanese occupation and then the allied bombings to liberate us destroyed so much of the city that our ancestors built. As you can see, the war was the main reason why we have very small collection of old structures left. that remind us, Cebuanos, and other people of our reputation as the "oldest city in the Philippines" But lets not dwell too much on this sad memory I still have to show many parts of my house. Let's go over there! 

这张地图的最后一层展示了我们城市在二战时期的经历。在长达三年的日军占领后，盟军的解放轰炸几乎摧毁了我们祖先精心建造的城市。正如你所看到的，正是这场战争，让我们如今仅存的历史建筑寥寥无几。然而，这些遗迹依然承载着记忆，提醒着宿务人以及所有游客，我们是“菲律宾最古老的城市”。不过，让我们别过多沉浸在这段沉重的往事中，我还有很多地方要带大家参观。来，我们往那边走吧！

### Stop 6

We are part of the house called 'zaguan' or 'silong'. This place held a lot of mystery for me when I was growing up. My mother would sternly admonish me whenever she noticed me venturing here. At times bales of abaca were piled here and at other times tobacco in bundles or barrels from our farm in the town of Barili. The smell of dried tobacco leaves circulating the house sticking even to our beddings still clings to memory. but I grew accustomed to it, and as I matured, I came to be more aware of the value of this dank, seemingly humble part of the house. My family grew and traded cash crops for our main source of income. It was the primary occupation of most Parian families for many, many years. Even as members of our community learned to engage in other professions and businesses the trade-in cash crops continued to be a profitable enterprise. During summertime, our parents would take us, their children, to our farms located far away from the city. those were very happy occasions for us! In those days, there was a great distinction between the urban and the rural areas. it was such a delight for us, who grew up in the confines of the city, to be able to explore to open countryside. I was able to see and try out myself the labors that produced those crops that afforded us our privileged city life. we would also go to the river to cool down or bathe in the sea. I loved taking rides in barotos out in the open sea. Sometimes, my siblings and I were even allowed to steer the barotos with paddles called bugsay. Oh, how time flies! As I grew up, I came to be deeply aware of the important role. Parian households played in the economic growth not only of the city but also in the rural areas. through our commercial activities, we linked farmers and other agricultural producers, to merchants in the city and abroad.

我们现在所在的是房子的一部分，被称为“zaguan”或“silong”。在我童年时，这里对我来说总是充满神秘感。每当母亲发现我偷偷溜进来，总会严厉地训斥我。有时，这里堆满了成捆的马尼拉麻；有时，则是来自我们巴里利镇农场的烟草，或成束，或装在大桶里。干烟叶的气味弥漫在整个房子里，甚至渗透到我们的床单上，至今仍清晰留存在我的记忆中。但随着时间的推移，我不仅习惯了这股气味，更开始理解这个潮湿而不起眼的地方所承载的价值。

种植与贸易经济作物是我家族的主要收入来源，Parian的许多家庭世代从事这一行业。即便后来社区成员逐渐涉足其他职业和商业，经济作物贸易依然是一项盈利的事业。每年夏天，父母都会带我们远离城市，回到乡间的农场，那是我们最快乐的时光！当时，城市与乡村之间的区别非常明显，对于在城市里长大的我们来说，能踏足广阔的乡野无疑是一种莫大的享受。我亲眼见证并亲身体验了这些经济作物的种植和收获，而正是这些作物，支撑着我们在城市里的优渥生活。

当然，我们的快乐远不止于此。我们会去河里消暑，或是到大海里畅游。我尤其喜欢乘坐“barotos”——当地的小船，在开阔的海面上尽情航行。有时，父母还会让我们用“bugsay”桨来掌舵，体验驾驶的乐趣。哦，时光荏苒！随着成长，我逐渐深刻意识到，Parian 的家庭不仅推动了城市的繁荣，也促进了乡村的发展。通过商业贸易，我们将农民和农业生产者与城市乃至海外的商人紧密联系起来，成为经济循环中不可或缺的一部分。

### Stop 7

There are so many things one can see in the silong of the house! This was also where we kept our pasos and carrozas. Pasos are the sculptures that are taken out for procession when the community celebrates religious events. They mounted onto carrozas like the one that you see here. The Cebu that I grew up was deeply religious. There was not a month when a fiesta patron or some other religious celebration was not held. Aside from that, there were daily devotionals. I can still see some of that religious culture to this day. Our household was very particular about our religious traditions as my grand uncle was a bishop. We will talk about him more later. For now, let me tell you some things about our house traditions. In January, we have the Sinulog dance prayer performed in our garden as a way of remembering our departed loved ones. Cebu is known for the Sinulog festival today. Its beginnings can be traced to this traditional dance form which is performed here in Cebu and other parts of the Visayas and Mindanao by local dance troupes. During Semana Santa or Holy Week, our pasos of Santa Veronica and the Segunda Caida or Second Fall of Jesus are taken for the Cathedral's processions. The entire Philippines stops for this sacred tradition lasting several days and the processions become the gathering points of communities. We also have a paso that depicts the baptism of Christ by Saint John. Do you remember the church of Parian? We still hold the fiesta of our parish that was dissolved in 1849. We still hold a traditional procession with Casa Gorordo's San Juan pasos as the centerpiece. This is one way by which we conserve traditions that are important not only to our family but also to our community.

房子的底层藏着许多值得一看的东西！这里曾是我们存放 pasos 和 carrozas 的地方。Pasos 是宗教雕像，在社区举行宗教庆典时会被抬出游行，而这些雕像通常会安放在 carrozas 上，就像你眼前看到的这辆一样。

我成长的宿务是一座笃信宗教的城市。几乎每个月都会有节日庆典，纪念守护神或其他宗教活动。此外，每天还有固定的祷告仪式。即使到了今天，我仍能感受到这种深厚的宗教氛围。我们的家庭对信仰尤为重视，特别是因为我的叔公曾是一位主教（稍后我们会更多谈到他）。

现在，让我先为你介绍一些我们家的传统习俗。每年一月，我们都会在花园里跳 Sinulog 祈祷舞，以缅怀逝去的亲人。如今，宿务因盛大的 Sinulog 节而闻名，而这一节庆正是源于这种传统的舞蹈仪式。如今，在宿务及整个维萨亚斯群岛和棉兰老地区，许多当地舞蹈团依然在传承这一文化传统。

在圣周（Semana Santa）期间，我们会抬着圣维罗尼卡（Santa Veronica）的 paso 和描绘耶稣第二次跌倒（Segunda Caida）的 paso 参加大教堂的游行。整个菲律宾都会在这几天暂停日常事务，投入到这一神圣的宗教仪式中，而这些游行也成为社区民众聚集的重要时刻。此外，我们还有一座 paso，描绘了圣约翰为耶稣施洗的场景。

你还记得 Parian 教堂吗？尽管这个教区早在 1849 年便已被解散，但我们至今仍然庆祝它的节日，并坚持举行传统游行，而 Casa Gorordo 的 San Juan paso 便是整个庆典的核心。这不仅是我们家庭信仰传承的一部分，更是社区共同守护的重要文化遗产。


### Stop 8
The zaguan is sometimes referred to as a corridor, but as you can see here, it also serves as a garage! In rural areas, I saw that they also put pens for chickens, pigs and goats in their silong. In the city, we don't really do that but instead, keep our tartanillas or horse-drawn carriage here. In farming communities they had caromata and balsas pulled by either carabaos or cows and the animals were tethered to the house posts at the silong. Whenever we went to the province, I always looked forward to riding the caromata or the balsa. The rides were slow and bumpy on those rocky, muddy roads, jolted my bones to the core but I enjoyed them a lot! I belonged to a generation who was fortunate to see and take a trip in Cebu trains. I remember very well those long train rides going Argao in the south or Danao in the north The city's central station was but a kilometer away from our house the train whistle, which sounded like a whisper from our windows seemed so serene. However. The central station when you're actually there, is as noisy as the busiest market. the train, chugging in clanking and screaming through its smoking pipe was like a metal beast barreling through the city when it arrived or left the station. Standing in the midst of the chattering crowd waiting for our turn to ride the train never failed to stir my spirit. Later on, my family bought a car, and it was parked here. So much has changed in Cebu's streetscape. Back then, there were only very few privately owned automobiles. traffic was light and getting from one point to the city to another took no time. The streets were still not cemented or asphalted, and in the rainy months, the potholes formed easily like sludge. But I couldn't complain. I knew we enjoyed a privilege only a few had. Layer upon layer of paving was added to our streets over time such that we could no longer park the car inside the silong as it sank lower. Luckily, we had a yard beside the house that we could convert into a "garaje."

Zaguan 有时被称为走廊，但如你所见，它其实还能当作车库使用！在乡下，我见过人们在 silong（屋下空间）里养鸡、养猪，甚至养山羊。而在城市，我们通常不会这么做，而是把 tartanilla（宿务传统马车）停放在这里。在农耕地区，人们使用 caromata（双轮马车）和 balsa（木筏），这些交通工具由水牛或黄牛拉动，而牲畜则拴在房屋 silong 的柱子上。

每次回乡，我总是期待能坐上 caromata 或 balsa。虽然这些车行驶在坑洼泥泞的乡间小道上，颠簸得让我浑身酸痛，但那种体验却让我兴奋不已！我是少数见证过并乘坐过宿务火车的一代人。我依然清楚地记得，那些沿铁路线驶往南方阿尔高（Argao）或北方达瑙（Danao）的漫长旅程。

宿务的中央火车站距离我家仅一公里，火车汽笛声传入窗内，仿佛低声细语，显得宁静而悠远。然而，真正置身车站，却会发现那里如同最热闹的市场，嘈杂无比。火车进站时，车轮撞击铁轨发出震耳欲聋的声响，烟囱喷吐着浓烟，仿佛一头咆哮的钢铁巨兽，在城市中穿梭。站在人潮拥挤的站台上，等待列车到来的那一刻，总能让我热血沸腾。

后来，我们家买了一辆汽车，最初停放在 silong 里。但宿务的街景早已今非昔比。当时，私人汽车极为罕见，交通顺畅，城市内出行几乎不费时间。雨季时，道路坑洼泥泞，但我无怨无悔——毕竟，能拥有一辆汽车仍是一种难得的特权。

随着城市发展，街道不断翻修，silong 逐渐变低，最终无法再停放汽车。幸运的是，我们家旁边有个院子，后来改建成了车库（garaje）。

### Stop 9

You must have heard about Cebu's moniker as the Queen City of the South Philippines. It's because of our status as the second largest metropolis, next to Manila, the capital. Even before the Europeans came, there had been a big settlement here because our port was a magnet for maritime commerce. But there was a long period when Cebu's Economy declined because its port was closed to foreign trade. Spanish Colonial Authorities shut all Philippine ports, except Manila, soon after they settled, to tighten control of trading activities in the colony. As a result, once thriving ports, like Cebu, lost their economic stature. However, due to pressures of the new capitalist world in order the 1800's, Spain was forced to open provincial ports to world trade. Cebu was reopened in the 1860's Parian Families were quick to flex their business acumen. People rode the waves of change having little awareness that the economic boom would also shatter the old colonial order. This was the time when my great-grandfather came to the Philippines. I often imagined what Cebu was like when he came here in the century past. Was the port area filled with all sorts of people? The piles of cargo from various parts of the Visayas and Mindanao must have choked the dock waiting for them to be boarded onto steamships that would take them to other parts of the world. I was always curious if women were as strong and could carry heavy loads adeptly as the men who worked the docks. Today, Cebu's port area stretches for at least five kilometers. I read somewhere that 80% of interisland shipping in the country is Centered here. The port, then, must be directly connected to the city's fortunes throughout our history.

Now we are at the part of the house called the azotea. Modern houses in the Philippines today don't have this extension anymore. It served a lot of purposes. Laundry was done here sometimes and ironing of clothes too. But it’s the leisurely activities done here that I remember fondly. We would have long conversations here in the afternoons. munching on refreshments. Do you see the martabhan jars lined along the windows? these were usually filled with water to cool down the warm air entered the house. Cebu must have been cooler then, I guess, as we didn’t require air conditioners and electric fans were not used often. Most gatherings we had in Casa Gorordo were done here in the evenings when a nice cool breeze helped everyone relax. The view of the garden below is also quite pleasing. We always took pride in having a well-maintained garden. Ornamental plants like San Francisco, hasmin, sampaguita, and the bombil grew lush in it. The flowering trees were quite lovely: kalachuchi, bauhinia, and magnolia champaca. There was also a time of the year when the flower of the angilan tree would fill the night air with the most alluring scent. You must have noticed this vine Crawling all over the azotea's trellis. It's called the bridal bouquet because of the tiny white blossoms that it produces when it is trimmed right. The blossoms appear like wisps of cloud caught in the trellis when they grew plenty. This vine has been clinging here as far as I can remember. My Tia Teling told me once it was planted when the house had just been built. So, this vine must be more than a century old! From here, beyond the garden and across the street, one could see our family's cuadra. We preferred to keep our horse and the carruaje or carriage there as most times, the zaguan was cramped in the dark. We had the cuadra taken down a few years the war when carruajes were thoroughly replaced by automobiles. Houses were built in place of it as more families moved into Parian. We end our tour of my house here at the azotea. I hope you enjoyed your visit of Casa Gorordo. I am so pleased that I could share with you, something of the story of our family and also of old Parian and of Cebu. I do hope this house visit gave you a better understanding of Cebuanos and our culture. Please tell your friends about us. I would be very happy to give them a tour of Casa Gorordo. I will leave you here to linger a little more. I do need to go back in as I hear someone at the front door downstairs. Another group of visitors perhaps? Haha! In Cebu we don't just say farewell when friends leave, we say, "do come back," or in Cebuano, "balik-balik!"
