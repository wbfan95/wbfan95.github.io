假期去新加坡旅游，去了动物园。当地寸土寸金，动物园里竟然五脏俱全。

游览车 tram 的语音蛮有趣的，车上有多种语言提供，但是要自带耳机，索性就把英语原文录下来了。

下面是讲解的文本，有些地方听不清楚标记了问号。


## Intro

Welcome aboard! This tram ride takes you on a delightful journey into the world of wild places.
It gives you an overview of our 26-hectare zoo and highlights some of our animals and zones. If you wish to have the full Singapore Zoo experience, we recommend you to also explore our path on foot in addition to the tram ride. For your safety, please remain seated while the tram is in motion. Keep your hands and the rest of you inside the vehicle.

Singapore Zoo is home to more than 4,200 animals representing over 300 species, of which 34% are threatened. We provide our animals with the best possible care, from nutrition, veterinary care, naturalistic housing, to enrichment activities to keep them physically and mentally healthy.

If you look towards your right, you'll see the Shore Foundation Amphitheatre. This is where you can enjoy our animal presentations twice daily. Weather permitting, you get to meet our animal stars and be awed by their natural behaviors. 

If you go up the slope on your left, you will reach Primate Kingdom. This is where our monkeys and lemurs play, rest, and forage in their naturalistic exhibits. Discover a wide range of trees, wild grasses, gingers, palms, and bamboos that are planted to provide lush greenery and to recreate the primate's natural habitat. 

We're approaching Elephants of Asia, situated next to the banks of the Upper Seletar Reservoir. This exhibit is home to our herd of five female Asian elephants. Each of them has unique appearance and personality. Make sure you get to know them by reading about our herd of elephants at the exhibit.
To promote natural foraging behavior, our keepers have installed multiple food puzzles around the exhibit. One even dispenses hasty treats like various types of nuts at random intervals. Try spotting these devices when you visit our elephants. In the wild, Asian elephants are facing severe threats from habitat encroachment, which results in human-elephant conflicts. Through our conservation arm, Mandai Nature, we support various field projects to mitigate human-elephant conflicts in Southeast Asia.

### Stop 2

We are approaching Tram Stock 2. From this Tram Stock, you may wish to explore Primary Kingdom on foot or to view the Hamadryas Baboons at the Great Rift Valley of Ethiopia.

We have reached Tram Stock 2. Disembark only after we have come to a complete stop. Thank you.

Welcome to our tram ride around Singapore Zoo. We are leaving Tram Stock 2.
For your safety, please remain seated while the tram is in motion. Keep your hands and the rest of you inside the vehicle.
On your left is the Great Rift Valley of Ethiopia. Home to Hamadryas baboons, Nubian ibexes and rock hyraxes.
We are crossing into Australasia Zone on your right. Try spotting a tall flightless bird with black feathers and a bright blue neck and face. This is the Kasawari. Its hair-like feathers allow rainwater to roll off the bird's body instead of weighing it down. This is useful for living in rainforests.
Besides the green kangaroos and agile wallabies from Australia, you get to see the tree kangaroos from Papua New Guinea. Tree kangaroos have powerful and strong limbs built for climbing. Their rubbery soles and curved claws allow for a better grip on trees. 
The Goodfellow's tree kangaroo is severely threatened in the wild. We work with other zoos to pair genetically compatible tree kangaroos for breeding. In this way, we help to sustain a healthy population of the tree kangaroo under human care. We are proud to share that our female Goodfellow's tree kangaroo, Nupela, has given birth to two joeys. 

Another animal which we have bred successfully is the pygmy hippopotamus. Pygmy hippos are only about half as tall as their cousins, the Nile hippos, and only a quarter of their cousins' weight. 

The path on your right will bring you to Asia's apex jungle predator, the tigers. With the strength of about 30 men, helping adult tigers can take on prey weighing up to a thousand kilograms. 

Poaching, habitat loss, and human-tiger conflicts have driven the Malayan tiger to bring the extinction, with fewer than 300 of them left in the wild. We work directly with the Malaysian Conservation Alliance of Tigers, or MI-CAT, to protect tigers and their homes. You can participate in its catwalk program and join forest rangers on their anti-poaching patrols. To find out more about our contribution to tiger conservation and other projects, please visit www.mandaination.org. 

Look up at the trees to view our free-ranging ormatons. Found in the rainforests of Borneo and Sumatra, ormatons are the largest tree-playing animals. Their feet with dexterous toes are great for grasping branches, and their long wings help them reach across the treetop canopy. Our free-ranging ormaton exhibit allows the animals to swing, climb, and explore in the treetops, as they would in the wild. 

## Stop 3

We will soon be reaching Tram Stop 3. If you wish to explore wild Africa to see our white rhinos, giraffes, and lions, disembark at the next stock. 
We have reached Tram Stop 3. Disembark only after we have come to a complete stop.

Welcome to our Tram Ride around Singapore zoo. We are leaving tram stop 3. For your safety, please remain seated while the tram is in motion. Keep your hands and the rest of you inside the vehicle.

On your right is our herd of white rhinoceroses. Rhinos are well known for their horns, which they use for posturing, territorial battles, and self-defense. We are very proud of our ongoing successful breeding program for white rhinos. We have sent many of our rhinos to overseas zoos to increase their number and diversify the rhino gene pool under human care. 

Back on your left is the world's fastest land animal. The cheetah has a flexible spine, long legs, sharp claws, and footpad bridges to help it reach an incredible speed of 115 kilometers per hour.

Giraffes are the tallest animals in the world, with the (top males ???) reaching a height of five and a half meters. Giraffes use this height to reach leaves of tall trees that other animals are not able to do so. Their long and flexible tongue allows them to strip leaves from branches. Our keepers use a pulley system to lift fresh leaves to a height that is natural and comfortable for our giraffes to feed.

If you look across the tram route, you'll see our African lion exhibit. Lions live in social groups known as prides, made up of wonder or monitor males, several closely related females, and their young. The lionesses do most of the hunting, while the male protects the pride from (her meals ???) and rival males.

Lions are also the only cat that sports a mane. The mane is believed to protect the male's throat during territorial fights with rival males.

We will be coming across Kid's World on your right. Kid's World is where children can discover the wonderful world of animals and learn how to care for them. Your new family can befriend the amazing animals that live here through animal interactions and fun activities. Then, explore the play trees and squish flash for a nature-inspired play. Kid's World is also the Ranger Waggles base camp, where children take on missions to practice life skills and learn to be a hero to themselves, animals, and the planet. There's always something happening at Kid's World.
Coming up on your left is Fragile Forest.

One of the highlights here is our 20,000 cubic meter walkthrough biojournal that is home to a wide array of animals from rainforests around the world. Your journey starts from the forest floor, where frogs and vertebrates thrive. Walk through the butterfly aviary before stepping into the danceforest for an immersive experience that makes your senses.

Keep your eyes peeled for surprises in every corner where you enjoy the songs of the forest. You must make your way up to the viewing platform of your breathtaking view of the forest canopy, where our Malayan flying foxes and birds roost and feed. This is where you are most likely to spot our blue-tailed lemurs and sloths.

## Stop 4

We have reached Tram Stop 4. If you wish to explore Fragile Forest and Kid's World, disembark here. You can also pay a visit to our Sotheby's Crested macaques and chimpanzees. 

Welcome to our tram ride around Singapore Zoo. We are leaving Tram Stop 4. We hope you had a wild time at Kid's World in Fragile Forest. 
For your safety, please remain seated while the tram is in motion. Keep your hands and the rest of you inside the vehicle.
We are coming across our troop of chimpanzees on the left. Chimpanzees are known for their intelligence and dynamic social structures. Using logs, vines, and upcycling items, we created a multi-tiered platform structure to encourage our chimps to exercise, play, and socialize as they would in the wild. Our keepers have also installed multiple food puzzles around the exhibit for our chimpanzees to explore. These food puzzles challenge the apes to problem solve, keeping them mentally stimulated.

Welcome to our tram ride around Singapore Zoo. As you explore the zoo, you are likely to encounter another intelligent primate, the long-tailed macaques. These wild monkeys are native to Singapore.

As you explore the zoo, you are likely to encounter another intelligent primate. The long-tailed macaques. These wild monkeys are native to Singapore. Though they are not under our care, they have made our park their home. They are habituated to the presence of humans and associate us with food. For your safety, observe them from a safe distance of at least 3 meters away. Do not feed them and keep plastic bags and food containers out of their sight. 
On the left is Reptile Kingdom, home to one of the world's most critically endangered turtles. The burmese roofed turtle was believed to be extinct until it was rediscovered in 2001. Working with conservationists, researchers, and zoos globally, we are privileged to care for an issuance colony of this species. Our hope is to eventually reintroduce this beautiful reptile back to its native habitat in Myanmar.

Named after our famous orangutan, Ah Meng restaurant and bistro is just a short walk away from Tran Stop 1. Apart from casual dining, this is also where breakfast in the wild is served daily. Find out more about this dining experience on our website. At Mandai, we believe that our daily choices make a difference to the future of our planet. Our Food and Beverage (F&B) outlets serve sustainable seafood and use sustainably sourced palm oil for cooking. We invite you to savor a cup of wildlife-friendly OWA coffee at Ah Meng Bistro. Your purchase will help us support farmers growing the coffee. We hope that through our actions, we can inspire more to embrace a sustainable lifestyle and reduce our impact on the environment. 

We have reached Tran Stop 1. Disembark only after we have come to a convinced stop. You can reach Ah Meng restaurant and bistro, the Shaw Foundation Amphitheatre, the Orangutans and Dipton Kingdom from this stop. This is also the closest Tran Stop to the exit.
